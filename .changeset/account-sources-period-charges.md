A host that charges indexing by the period names the period's end in its allowance: `indexingPeriodEndsAt`, the first millisecond after the period, as integer UTC epoch milliseconds. With it named, the store keeps one charge record for each source it indexed in the period, in a table `sourceSyncSchema` creates as its ninth and tenth statements (the table and its index by period end), so a host runs its migration again to add it; a database without the table, or a host that names no period, is charged as the release before charged it. The record holds the provenance of the newest text the store wrote or refreshed for the source in the period, message ids, spans, counts and hashes and never text, and it outlives the source's copy until the period ends. A read is weighed against it as well as against the copy the store holds, and charged the smaller amount: a thread whose copy's cache lifetime ended, read again unchanged in the same period, is stored again at no charge, and a reply to it is charged its separator and text. Each such read was charged its whole text again, so a mailbox read again each day after its copies expired spent a month's allowance in days, and from then on every read was refused `indexing_limit` and purged with a deletion record, cycle after cycle, with nothing left stored.

An `indexing_limit` skip that leaves no copy records a deferral: while the period lasts, the connection keeps its generation and the allowance leaves no more room, `page` queues the source settled as the same skip, with no read, no purge and no deletion record, counted among the cycle's settled and skipped items. The source is read again once the period turns, the room grows or the generation moves. A record goes with its connection and its owner, with a `gone` read or a provider's skip, with an exclusion, and once its period has ended: `expire` and `sweepExpired` remove up to `limit` ended records after the copies. Treat the new table as content for access, retention and backups, as the documents table is.
